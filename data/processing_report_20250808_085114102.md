# Market Intelligence Summary

## Document Overview
This document is Gartner's "Magic Quadrant for Managed Network Services" report, dated October 14, 2024. It evaluates and positions vendors in the Managed Network Services (MNS) market, focusing on how enterprises leverage these services to optimize expenses while maintaining network and security operations quality. The report highlights the growing demand for AI/machine learning-enabled providers to enhance service delivery through automation. It defines MNS to include the management of LAN, WAN, and security functions, emphasizing the importance of a robust Service Delivery Platform (SDP).

Key themes include the increasing integration of Secure Access Service Edge (SASE) with SD-WAN, the anticipated role of generative AI (GenAI) in network configuration, and the evolving customer expectations for end-to-end visibility, automation, and improved customer experience. The report provides detailed analyses of various vendors, categorizing them as Leaders, Challengers, Visionaries, or Niche Players based on their "Ability to Execute" and "Completeness of Vision." It also outlines critical success factors such as service delivery quality, network automation, and customer experience, offering strategic planning assumptions and recommendations for I&O leaders making sourcing decisions.

## Products & Services
- **Managed Network Services (MNS):** Externally provided network operations center (NOC) functionality, along with relevant network and security life cycle services (mentioned in document).
- **Core MNS Offerings:**
    - **Managed LAN services (MNS for LAN):** Management of enterprise LAN customer premises equipment (CPE), including campus switches and wireless access points, with single point of contact (SPOC) ownership for life cycle management. May include management of IoT/IIoT infrastructure and endpoints, on-premises servers, storage, gateways, and controllers (mentioned in document).
    - **Managed WAN services (MNS for WAN):** Management of site edge ingress and egress CPE, WAN connections, and service operations. Includes life cycle management for site edge CPE (routers, firewalls, SD-WAN), with or without security co-residency, and SPOC ownership. Supports hybrid cloud and non-client-owned locations. May include operations management of enterprise customer IoT/IIoI infrastructure and endpoint management (mentioned in document).
    - **Managed Security (MNS for Security):** Health, configuration, and maintenance support for security technologies, supporting single-vendor to multi-vendor converged network and security function life cycle management. Includes support for SD-WAN embedded security, secure web gateways (SWG), cloud access security brokers (CASB), network access control (NAC), and network firewalling (with or without IPS/IDS). Supports branch offices, remote workers, and on-premises internet security, private application access, and cloud service security (mentioned in document).
- **Service Delivery Platform (SDP):** Integrated application architecture and enabling technologies for standardized, high-quality, and scalable delivery of MNS. May be separate from but integrated with a security-specific MNS SDP (mentioned in document).
- **Common Features:**
    - Service Management (tool-based workflows, automation, customer support) (mentioned in document).
    - Operations Automation (for SDP, service management, and customer experience) (mentioned in document).
    - Support for Customer Endpoints (beyond network/security, including servers, storage, power, environmental, physical security, OT, IIoT) (mentioned in document).
    - Formal Continual Service Improvement Programs (mentioned in document).
    - Networking and Security Architecture Design Services (mentioned in document).

## Work Methods and Deliverables
- **Service Delivery Process:** MNS providers deliver services with internal employee resources. Processes must be repeatable, standardized, and automated for quality. ITIL framework adaptation is an example of process discipline (mentioned in document).
- **Operational Workflows and Methodologies:** Focus on automation for incident resolution and service request management. AI/ML is leveraged for automation orchestrations. Use of bots for automation of configuration and event management tasks. Emphasis on continual service improvement and collaboration with customer IT operations teams (mentioned in document).
- **Specific Deliverables:** MNS for LAN, WAN, and Security services. Management of network operations life cycle. Use of a multitenant SDP. Support for various network and security technologies (mentioned in document).
- **Quality Control Measures:**
    - Minimum average of 80% FCR for all incidents (manual or automated) (mentioned in document).
    - Minimum average of 20% FCR for all incidents via automation (zero manual touch) (mentioned in document).
    - High degree of process discipline, repeatable, standardized, automated processes (mentioned in document).
    - Data accuracy and integrated service management (mentioned in document).
    - Incident classification accuracy, zero-touch incident automation, and high-data quality management (mentioned in document).
- **Technology Stack and Tools Used:**
    - **Service Delivery Platforms (SDPs):** Multitenant hardware, software, applications, security, and scalability components for delivering MNS.
    - **APIs and Integrations:** Commonly integrate with customer ITSM tools (e.g., ServiceNow, BMC Helix) and APM tools (e.g., AppDynamics, Dynatrace, New Relic).
    - **Monitoring:** SNMP for endpoint and link monitoring. Ingestion of flow data (e.g., IPFIX).
    - **Automation Tools:** Thousands of bots used for automation, especially for configuration, event management, and AI/ML capabilities. Overlay network automation orchestration platforms, message bus/bot workflow frameworks, run book/playbook technologies.
    - **Core Tools:** May be integrated and automated from COTS vendors or be original tool developments, enhanced with overlay automation orchestration platforms.
    - **CMDB:** Accurate CMDB of customer's production networking estate is a mission-critical requirement.
    - **Customer Portals:** Always-available customer portals for interactions, providing near-real-time updates on service requests, incidents, and performance.
- **Project Management/Execution:** Onboarding typically runs 8-12 weeks but can vary. Enterprises undertaking transformational initiatives should select MNS providers first and stabilize the current estate before participating in transformation (mentioned in document).

    - **Customer Experience Monitoring:** Shift towards analyzing data for insights and proactive monitoring, aiming for anomaly detection (mentioned in document).
    - **Private 5G (P5G) and Wi-Fi 6:** Gaining traction, with many providers supporting these technologies and increased IoT/IIoT support (mentioned in document).

## Key Insights for RAG System

*   **AI/GenAI is the primary driver of future MNS market differentiation and competitive advantage.** Focus on providers with strong AI/ML roadmaps and current implementations for automation, incident resolution, and predictive analytics.
*   **Security convergence (SASE/SSE) with MNS is a critical trend.** Intelligence on provider capabilities in NAC, SWG, CASB, and firewall management, especially within SD-WAN, is essential for competitive analysis.
*   **Customer Experience (CX) and Service Delivery Platform (SDP) capabilities are key differentiators.** Monitor portal features, ease of navigation, real-time updates, and proactive anomaly detection as indicators of quality.
*   **Automation is paramount for service quality and cost efficiency.** Track key metrics like First Contact Resolution (FCR) and incident cycle times, especially those achieved through automation.
*   **Provider agnosticism (carrier and OEM) is highly valued by enterprise clients.** Evaluate provider claims of neutrality and their ability to support diverse environments.
*   **Pricing transparency and predictable cost models are critical.** Downward pricing trends for network hardware and services are influencing MNS pricing. Understand what is included in MNS pricing to avoid unexpected costs.
*   **Vendor-specific strengths and weaknesses are granular.** For each competitor, understand their specific advantages (e.g., specific industry focus, integration capabilities) and disadvantages (e.g., immature security offerings, poor portal experience) to inform targeting and competitive strategies.
*   **The market is shifting towards standardized, volume-based operations.** Providers that can deliver highly automated, repeatable services are positioned for success. Customization can lead to higher costs and lower efficiency.
*   **The definition of MNS is expanding to include more IoT/OT and private 5G capabilities.** Providers' ability to manage these diverse endpoints is becoming increasingly important.
*   **Gartner Magic Quadrant positioning (Leaders, Challengers, Niche Players) provides a high-level competitive benchmark.** Use this as a starting point for deeper analysis of individual vendor strengths and weaknesses.
